Channels and Easing

Every preset is a list of channels.

A channel animates one property of the target for a short, tuned burst; a preset layers several channels so the flash, squash, shake, and hit-stop of a single hit land together, already balanced against each other.

Common channel fields

Every channel carries the same base fields:

  • Delay — seconds before this channel starts, so channels inside one preset can stagger.
  • Duration — seconds the channel runs.
  • Mode — Punch or To. Punch eases out and back, returning exactly to the base value at both ends. To eases one way toward the target value.
  • Ease — the easing curve used by To mode and by shake decay.
  • RestoreOnEnd — when true (the default), the channel writes back the base state it captured when the cue began. Flash and Shake always restore. To-mode channels authored for exits and rising popups are the named exceptions, with restore off on purpose.

When a cue begins, each channel captures the target's base state. When the effect ends or is stopped, the captured state is written back — a stopped or finished effect does not leave a target squashed, tinted, offset, or rotated.

The seven channels

  • Scale — Amount is a Vector3 multiplier. A per-axis amount is the squash-and-stretch form: widen one axis while flattening the other.
  • Position — Offset is a Vector3 in local space. Used for lunges, recoils, dips, and rises.
  • Rotation — EulerAmount is degrees per axis. Tilts, spins, and camera kicks.
  • Color — TargetColor, applied to the target's SpriteRenderer or uGUI Graphic. A punch moves the color toward the target and back.
  • Flash — FlashColor, Strength, and AttackPortion. The fast bright overlay used for hits; it rises to the flash color at its attack peak and always restores the base color.
  • Shake — Amplitude, Frequency, and an Axes mask. The offset stays within the amplitude on masked-in axes, masked-out axes do not move, and the base position is restored at the end. List a shake after a position channel when both run in one preset, since channels apply in preset order.
  • HitStop — StopDuration in unscaled seconds and StopTimeScale. It requests the stop on its first tick and completes immediately. See JuicePlayer & Cues for how overlapping stops combine.

Channels hold no playing state. Each play rents a runner for the channel; the runner captures base state in its begin step, advances per tick, and restores when it finishes.

Easing curves

29 curves, evaluated by a pure static evaluator with no allocation:

  • Linear
  • Sine, Quad, Cubic, Quart, Quint, Expo, Circ, and Back — each in In, Out, and InOut forms (24 curves)
  • Elastic — In and Out
  • Bounce — In and Out

Every curve evaluates to 0 at the start and 1 at the end. OutBack overshoots above 1 mid-curve, which is where the spring in UI pops comes from. Punch mode uses its own out-and-back envelope, so a punch returns exactly to base regardless of the Ease field; the Ease field shapes To mode and shake decay.
