Quick Start

From import to a prototype that feels shipped, in about five minutes.

1. Open the window

Tools > ByteLura > JuiceWorks Pro. The welcome block holds the four actions you will actually use: open the sample, verify the install, documentation, support. Click Verify Install once — it checks the assemblies, the preset catalog, and the engine itself, and reports each check as one plain-language line in the header.

2. Feel the sample

Click Open the Juice Arcade. The sample scripts import into Assets/Samples/JuiceWorks Pro/1.0.0/Juice Arcade; follow its README to assemble the scene (about two minutes — see the Sample Guide for the full build), then press Play and tap the targets. Every reaction you see is a built-in preset, played by name.

3. Juice your own object

  1. Select any sprite or UI element in your scene.
  2. Add a JuicePlayer component (Add Component > ByteLura > JuiceWorks Player).
  3. In the JuiceWorks window, pick a shelf and click Preview next to a preset — it plays on your selected object right in the Editor, without entering Play Mode.
  4. Found your feel? Play it from your game:
    • No code: add a JuiceTrigger, pick the moment (pointer down, collision, enable), type the preset name. Done.
    • One line:
    GetComponent<JuicePlayer>().Play("HitMedium");

4. Chain effects into sequences

Create > ByteLura > JuiceWorks Sequence. Add steps in the timeline — each step is a preset, a delay, and an optional “wait for previous”. Preview on any selected object, then play the asset from a JuiceSequencer component or a trigger. See the Sequencer page.

5. Tune or author your own presets

Create > ByteLura > JuiceWorks Preset. Add channels (Scale, Position, Rotation, Color, Flash, Shake, Hit-Stop), tune the numbers, preview. The built-in presets are plain data too — open the Preset Library to see what each one is made of, copy the values, make them yours.

If something looks wrong at step 1

With no GameObject selected, the preset browser shows its empty-selection line — “Select a GameObject in the Hierarchy to preview presets on it.” — and preview buttons stay disabled until you select one. If Verify Install reports a failing check, the line itself states what was checked in plain language.
